# Comfort & trust survey — run with real people

**Goal:** Learn what makes people comfortable entering their data and sharing it with a doctor, and whether the layout and colours help or hurt.

**Who:** 10–20 people. Ideally a mix of patients, family members who have supported someone through psychiatric care, and 2–3 doctors. Include people outside big cities and older family members if you can.

**Format:** 20–30 minutes each, in person or video call. Let them use the app on their own phone with the **fictional example** — never ask for real health details. Watch quietly; don't help unless they are stuck for more than a minute. Take notes on where they hesitate.

**Ethics:** Get verbal consent, say they can stop at any time, don't record names next to answers, and don't ask about their own diagnosis or medicines. If someone becomes distressed, pause, be kind, and share Tele-MANAS (14416).

---

## Part A — First impressions (before touching anything, 1 minute)

Show the home screen for 10 seconds, then ask:

1. In a few words, what do you think this app does?
2. How does it make you feel? (calm / neutral / uneasy / other: ___)
3. On a scale of 1–5, how much would you trust this app with private health information? Why?
4. Is there anything on the screen that puts you off?

## Part B — Tasks (watch, don't help)

5. "Imagine this is your mother's prescription. Add a visit from 15 February where she was feeling low, and add her medicines." *(Give them `docs/survey-materials/fictional-pharmacy-bill.png` printed or on a second screen to photograph.)*
   - Did they find the photo / paste options? ☐ yes ☐ no
   - Time taken: ___
   - Did they notice and check the "read from photo — please check" warnings? ☐ yes ☐ no
6. "Add a second visit where the medicines were the same." — Did they find *Same as last visit*? ☐ yes ☐ no
7. "Find out whether anything looks worth asking the doctor about."
8. "Show me what you would hand to a doctor."
9. "Make sure no one could tell who this is before you send it to a different doctor."

After each task: *"How easy was that, 1 (very hard) to 5 (very easy)?"*

## Part C — Comfort and trust (1–5, then "why?")

10. I felt comfortable entering this kind of information. 
11. I believe my information stays on my phone.
12. The language felt kind, not scary.
13. I understood the patterns it found.
14. I would show the Doctor summary to my doctor.
15. I would worry my doctor might be upset if I showed it. *(1 = not at all, 5 = very much)*

## Part D — Colour and layout comparison

Show the three screenshots in `docs/survey-materials/` (A: current teal/blue on warm white, B: all-blue on pure white, C: warm saffron/green) in a random order. For each:

16. Which feels most **calm**?
17. Which feels most **trustworthy** for health information?
18. Which would you be most **likely to keep using**?
19. Do any colours carry a meaning for you or your family that we should know about?

## Part E — Open questions

20. What would make you *more* likely to use this?
21. What would stop you from using it?
22. Would you prefer it in Tamil, Hindi, or another language?
23. *(Family members)* What do you wish you had known earlier when supporting someone?
24. *(Doctors only)* What would make this summary useful rather than annoying in a short appointment? What should never be on it?

---

## How to record and compare

Make a simple sheet with one row per person and columns for: role (patient / family / doctor), age range, city or town, each 1–5 score, task success yes/no and time, palette choices (A/B/C), and three short quotes.

**What "good" looks like for the next version:**
- Average comfort (Q10) and trust (Q11) of 4 or more
- At least 8 in 10 people complete the add-a-visit task without help
- Fewer than 2 in 10 worry about upsetting their doctor (Q15 ≥ 4) — if more, the Doctor summary wording needs work
- A clear palette preference (one palette chosen by most people for calm *and* trust)

Write the results into `docs/survey-results.md` and decide v0.4 changes from them.
