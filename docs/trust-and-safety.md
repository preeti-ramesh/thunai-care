# Trust & Safety principles

Thunai Care only works if people feel safe using it. Patients must never feel exposed, and good doctors must never feel hunted. These principles come before any feature, and every new feature is checked against them.

---

## 1. Anonymous by default

- **No real names needed.** A nickname is enough for the patient. Doctors are recorded as labels (A, B, C), only so the tool can see when someone changed doctors.
- **Keep only what the review needs.** From a prescription, the tool keeps medicines, doses and the gaps between visits. It does not keep names, phone numbers, addresses, hospital names or registration numbers.
- **Redact before anything is shared.** When prescription photos are supported (planned), identifying details are hidden *before* anything is saved or sent, and the patient checks the redacted version.
- **Relative dates when sharing.** Anything shared with a reviewer uses "Week 0, Week 6…" instead of calendar dates.
- **The patient holds the record.** Today everything stays in the patient's browser. Delete means delete.

## 2. Two separate tracks — never mixed

| | **Second opinion** (default) | **Raise a concern** (rare, opt-in) |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | "Is this care reasonable?" | "Something may have gone wrong." |
| Who starts it | The patient | Only the patient, by explicit choice |
| Precondition | None | An independent review supports the concern |
| Who sees the doctor's identity | Nobody — the case is anonymous | Only the proper authority, with the patient's consent |
| Where it goes | Back to the patient only | Proper channels: State Medical Council, Mental Health Review Board under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, legal aid |
| Public? | Never | Never |

**The platform itself never names, rates or shames an individual treating doctor in public.** Changing doctors is a private choice. Calling out wrongdoing is a formal process with due process for the doctor.

## 3. Comfort for everyone in the cycle

**Patients and families**
- Minimal typing: paste or (later) photograph a prescription; only feelings, side effects and observations are typed.
- Always see exactly what will be shared before it is shared.
- Language that never blames the patient for "not noticing sooner."
- Crisis help always visible (Tele-MANAS 14416).

**Independent reviewing doctors**
- Review anonymous cases, so they judge the *care*, not a colleague by name. This removes professional awkwardness and fear of defamation.
- Flat fee per review regardless of outcome; quality judged by accuracy (peer spot-checks), not by how many problems they find.
- A short, structured doctor summary instead of pages of patient notes.

**Treating doctors**
- Never exposed publicly.
- A reassuring second opinion protects their reputation.
- If a formal concern is ever raised, they are heard through a fair process run by the proper authority, not by this platform.

## 4. Honest limits

- **Anonymity is not perfect.** A rare medicine combination plus a small town and a timeline can sometimes identify a person. Relative dates and removing locations reduce this; a privacy expert must review before launch.
- **Reading prescriptions can go wrong**, especially handwriting. The patient always confirms what was read.
- **Patterns are not proof.** Flags are reasons to ask questions, not verdicts.

## 5. Before launch (checklist)

- [ ] Clinical review of red-flag rules
- [ ] Privacy review against India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023
- [ ] Legal review of the "raise a concern" track and of reviewer liability under the Telemedicine Practice Guidelines
- [ ] Tested with families who have been through this, for comfort and clarity
